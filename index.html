1. How to read a poem

Read it once for the feeling. Read it again for the meaning. Check the title, who is speaking (the speaker), and how the lines are grouped into stanzas.

Try it: “Quiet rain / taps the window like a friend.” The speaker feels calm and comforted.

2. Forms and structure

Haiku: 3 lines, 5-7-5 syllables. Free verse: no set rhyme or rhythm. Narrative poem: tells a story. A line break makes the reader pause. A rhyme scheme labels end rhymes with letters, so lines that rhyme share a letter (ABAB means lines 1 and 3 rhyme, and lines 2 and 4 rhyme).

3. Sound and figurative language

Alliteration: repeated beginning sounds. Repetition: a word or phrase used again for emphasis. Simile: compares with “like” or “as.” Metaphor: says one thing is another. Personification: gives human actions to a non-human thing. Imagery: words that create a picture for the senses.

4. Meaning, tone, and evidence

Tone is the speaker’s attitude. Mood is the feeling the poem gives the reader. For a theme question, decide the message first. Then find the lines that prove it.
